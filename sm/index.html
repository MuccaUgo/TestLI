Concepts

Each card gives the meaning, how the rule works in practice, and the typical exam trap.

High-frequency traps
Trap
Percentages need a valuation

A stake falling from 40% to 20% can be worth far more if the company grew from €10m to €60m. Never read dilution without the valuation behind it.

Trap
Post-money, not pre-money

An investor's share is the investment divided by the post-money valuation, which already includes the cash injected.

Trap
Primary ≠ secondary IPO

In a primary the cash goes to the company; in a secondary it goes to the selling shareholders.

Trap
SV = EV − debt + cash

Cash reduces net debt, so it raises shareholder value. Dropping the cash term is the usual slip.

Trap
Hostile refers to the board

A takeover is hostile when the target's board opposes it, whatever the price offered.

Trap
Unlimited ≠ unregulated

Unlimited companies lack limited liability but are exempted from filing accounts for public disclosure.

Trap
One-tier still supervises

A single board is not an absence of supervision: non-executives outnumber executives and some act as supervisors.

Trap
Stakeholder view adds, not replaces

The argument is that shareholder value alone is insufficient over the long term, not that it should be abandoned.
